# X6100 birdies: full range scan, 2026-11-15

`birdiescan full` (x6100_trx, GEN BIRDIE SCAN FULL RANGE; STATUS item 56): the radio's
whole RX range, 148.5 kHz - 75 MHz, in 36 kHz windows, 32 averaged spectrum frames each,
peaks at least 16 dB over the noise. Dummy load on the antenna port; started from the GEN
screen, display and WiFi on (not `quiet`); image and supply not recorded.

## Summary

- 1015 birdies from 79 kHz to 75 MHz (the list holds 4096: not full). 105 at 30 dB or
  more over the noise, 21 at 40 dB or more. Spread fairly evenly: 40-90 per 5 MHz.
- One source dominates: about **6395.68 kHz** and every harmonic of it, 1 to 11. Nine of
  them are 47-56 dB over the noise, the strongest birdies of the scan.
- Around those harmonics: sidebands every about **99.93 kHz** (149 birdies, 15 % of the
  list, on n x 6395.68 + m x 99.93 kHz within 0.3 kHz; by chance below 1 %), and close-in
  clusters 3.5-7 kHz apart whose spacing differs per harmonic.
- The **7.58 kHz comb** of the scans of 2026-11-06 / 07 (`x6100_xiegu_questions.md`
  item 1) is **gone**: 7005.7-7092.7 and 14041.7-14064.4 kHz (14064.4 kHz was 43 dB) are
  not in this list. The two strongest of then, 51165.4 and 70352.4 kHz, are still there
  at the same strength: the 8th and 11th harmonic of 6395.68 kHz.

## What the clock may be (hypothesis)

The FCC internal photos (FCC ID 2ANLH-X6100) show an ST STM32F4 (marking read as
32F427ZGT6, the third digit uncertain) on the base unit, next to two shielded cans; the
display unit has the Allwinner R16 module (Linux, the UI), WiFi / Bluetooth and USB.

The numbers fit the IQ reaching the STM32 over I2S: I and Q as left and right, 32-bit
slots, so a bit clock of 64 x the sample rate: 6395.68 kHz / 64 = **99.932 kHz**, the
width of the IQ (taken as 100 kHz so far, 512 bins). The word clock at 99.93 kHz is the
spacing of the sidebands; a master clock at 256 x would be 25.583 MHz, the 4th harmonic
(51 dB). Not proven. The flow frames (about 27.5 a second, 512 samples each) are
snapshots, so their rate does not show the sample rate; `spancal` measures the IQ
width from a carrier at +-40 kHz instead (expected: about -680 ppm if this holds;
method error under 70 ppm).

The Xiegu X6100 maintenance manual (version 1.0): zero IF (a quadrature local oscillator
at the tuned frequency), a **26 MHz TCXO** reference, and a **CS4272** (Cirrus Logic
24-bit stereo codec) as the ADC of the I / Q, into the STM32. At about 100 kHz the
CS4272 runs in double speed: bit clock 64 x Fs (6.3957 MHz), master clock 128 or 256 x Fs
(12.79 / 25.58 MHz: the two strongest harmonics, 56 and 51 dB).

No setting of the STM32F4 I2S PLL (with or without MCLK out, also through MCO2) gives
99.932 kHz from an 8, 12, 16, 25 or 26 MHz input; exactly 100 kHz is easy from all of
them (28 settings from 16 MHz). One explanation that fits: the audio clock nominally 100
kHz from the STM32's internal RC oscillator (HSI, 16 MHz, factory trimmed to about 1 %),
which runs 675 ppm low on this radio. The birdies are measured against the receiver (the
26 MHz TCXO), so the 675 ppm is a real difference; a crystal would not be that far off.
To check: `spancal` (about -680 ppm expected), and the harmonics against temperature (a
cold and a warm radio: an RC clock moves them by hundreds of Hz at 70 MHz, the dial
does not move).

**Measured 2026-11-15 (`spancal`, the 12791.331 kHz birdie, 128 spectra a side, 56.7 /
56.6 dB):** upper +205.0389, lower -204.8302 bins, distance 409.8691 (409.6000 at 100
kHz): **IQ rate 99934.4 Hz, -656 ppm.** The same as the offline check gave for a true
99932 Hz (99934.4): the IQ is 99.93 kHz wide, the codec clocks 64 x and 128 / 256 x that.
The HSI as their source is not proven yet (cold / warm).
